Reading the map

Top-left quadrant (green) is the sweet spot: navigators (or their employing institutions) that are both well-positioned to improve outcomes and have a direct financial incentive to do so. This is where a venture-backed NavTech company should sell. Bottom-left (purple) contains navigators who are excellent at the work but are funded by grants or government — they need a payor (typically an MCO or hospital) to fund the tool on their behalf. Top-right (tan) contains actors with money but less direct contact with the parent. Bottom-right (red) contains navigators with narrow positioning or misaligned incentives — important for the ecosystem but harder to build a business around. The payor flow diagrams in each detail card show the money path: who buys the tool, who deploys it, and who it ultimately serves.

This analysis is specific to the new parent benefits journey. Navigator positioning and financial incentives differ across other journeys (e.g., post-incarceration, disability onset). The payor models draw from NLV's Benefits Access Market Exploration (2026), particularly the working document on Problem and Opportunity Areas, the Early Thinking Writeup, and the field mapping of companies in the space. Relevant companies named are from NLV's existing research and represent the types of solutions that could serve each channel — not endorsements.
