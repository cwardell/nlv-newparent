NextLadder Ventures — New Parent Benefits Journey

State policy landscape

Which states make it easiest — and hardest — to close the new parent benefits gap?

Six policy dimensions determine how much of the $28K+ benefit bundle is accessible in a given state, and how viable it is to build a business serving new parents there. States are tiered by the combination of these dimensions. Click any state to see its specific policy profile. Highlighted states represent recommended launch markets.

Policy dimensions (hover for detail)
Medicaid expansion
Whether the state has expanded Medicaid to 138% FPL for all adults. Non-expansion states have extremely low income thresholds for non-pregnant adults (median ~38% FPL), creating a coverage cliff postpartum.
12-mo postpartum
Whether the state has adopted the permanent option to extend Medicaid postpartum coverage from 60 days to 12 months. 49 states + DC have adopted; only Arkansas has not.
State PFML
Whether the state has a mandatory Paid Family Medical Leave program paying benefits. 13 states + DC have or will soon have programs. In ~36 states without PFML, new parents get zero income replacement during leave.
SDOH / HRSN waiver
Whether the state has a Section 1115 waiver allowing Medicaid to pay for social-needs services (housing, food, navigation) through MCOs. This is the payor mechanism that funds the navigator workforce and creates a market for NavTech products.
MCO penetration
Whether the state delivers Medicaid primarily through managed care organizations (who have capitation-based financial incentive to keep members enrolled) vs. fee-for-service.
State EITC/credits
Whether the state has its own earned income tax credit or child tax credit, adding to the federal EITC/CTC. ~30+ states have some form of state credit. Increases the total bundle value and the ROI case for a screening tool.
Strategic implication

Launch in Tier 1 — California or New York — where the full bundle is available, the MCO payor channel is established through SDOH waivers, and the infrastructure exists to sell a benefits screening tool to an institutional buyer. A startup should prove its model in one of these states before expanding.

Expand to Tier 2 as those states build out SDOH infrastructure. North Carolina, Illinois, and Michigan are the highest-volume Tier 2 markets. Virginia and Pennsylvania may move to Tier 1 if PFML legislation passes.

Tier 3 and 4 represent the highest need but the hardest business environment. Texas alone accounts for 42% of the national Medicaid coverage gap. Maria, NLV's primary persona, lives in Houston. Serving these states may require a different model — grantmaking, government contracts, or federal-level solutions — rather than the MCO-sold SaaS that works in Tier 1. The irony is that the states where the gap is largest are the ones where the business infrastructure to close it doesn't yet exist.

Tiering is based on six policy dimensions as of March 2026. State positions can change — PFML legislation is active in several Tier 2 states (Virginia, Pennsylvania), and federal HR1 provisions (Medicaid work requirements beginning 2027) will affect all tiers. SDOH/HRSN waiver status reflects approved and actively operating programs; several additional states have pending or early-stage waivers not reflected here. MCO penetration is characterized as high (>60% of Medicaid beneficiaries in managed care), moderate (30–60%), or low (<30%). Medicaid-covered birth volumes are approximate based on NCHS 2024 data and state Medicaid share of births. "Recommended launch" highlights are based on the combination of policy environment, payor infrastructure, birth volume, and market readiness — not solely on need.
